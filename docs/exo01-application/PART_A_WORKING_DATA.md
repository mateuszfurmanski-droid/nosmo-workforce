## Abstract — draft for review

EXO-01 proposes to investigate a passive human-augmentation architecture for repetitive heavy-tool handling. The research will combine a full-body load-transfer structure, articulated tool support, modular tool coupling, frame-mounted tool-power routing and vibration isolation. Its central question is whether tool and power-source loads can be redirected while preserving operator mobility and control, without increasing harmful vibration or transferring excessive loads to other body regions. Existing Morph technology would be treated as background, subject to ownership and access verification; only genuinely new research components and their interaction would form the research object. A narrow drilling/fixing task will support modelling, bench experiments and controlled laboratory evaluation with surface EMG, motion and force measurements. Comparative tests will assess load distribution, muscle activation, movement, tool stability, accuracy, comfort and vibration. Expected outputs are a laboratory research demonstrator, validated models, a reproducible evaluation protocol and evidence of technical feasibility and limitations. No commercial deployment or injury-prevention claim is made. Proposed expertise spans mechanical exoskeleton engineering, construction workflows and independent biomechanics. Participation, resources and final objectives remain subject to partner agreement.
