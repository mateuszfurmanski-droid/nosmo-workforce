## 1. Excellence

### 1.1 Objectives and ambition

EXO-01 will investigate whether a passive full-body structure can redirect the combined load of a heavy tool and its power source while maintaining voluntary movement and accurate tool positioning. The scientific challenge is the coupled interaction between load transfer, human joint motion, support-arm behaviour and vibration propagation. Reducing shoulder effort alone may shift load to the pelvis, legs or trunk; a successful design must examine this redistribution rather than assume a net benefit.

The research object is a new construction/industrial tool-support architecture, not the commercialisation or artificial TRL reclassification of Morph. The existing platform, patents and relevant know-how remain background subject to verified ownership and agreed access. Candidate new components are the tool arm and interface, task-specific full-body load path, isolation arrangement and frame-mounted power routing. Their entry readiness and novelty have not yet been established.

### 1.2 Methodology

Start with task observation/interviews and a task taxonomy covering heavy concrete drilling, repeated fixing and overhead installation. Select one primary laboratory task to limit variation. Define tool, fixture, posture, reach, duty cycle and tool-power configuration. Keep representative construction constraints but do not describe laboratory replication as an operational construction pilot.

Build free-body and biomechanical models of the person–frame–tool system, with explicit joint alignment, friction, support forces and reaction loads. Compare alternative load paths, passive balancing mechanisms, module locations and isolation elements. Use sensitivity analysis across anthropometry and task posture, then calibrate against measured forces and displacements. Record model error and the conditions where predictions fail.

Bench testing precedes any human exposure: structural behaviour, support stability, release/failure behaviour, joint pinch/entanglement hazards, cable routing, mass distribution and vibration propagation. Instrument tool, frame and body-interface proxies. Passive assistance does not mean unpowered tools: tool battery safety and mass remain part of the research boundary. Tool electrical architecture and interface permissions require specialist confirmation.

For human studies, propose a within-participant comparison of baseline tool handling and EXO-01 assistance, with randomised/counterbalanced order, familiarisation and rest. Add a conventional-support comparator if feasible and informative. Standardise task geometry and tool settings; document posture and movement. Surface EMG must be normalised with a stated method; analyse selected muscle groups alongside motion, contact/ground forces and tool accuracy. Record perceived exertion, discomfort location, mobility, don/doff time and incidents. Cycle time is exploratory and must not encourage unsafe pace.

The research partner will choose the primary endpoint, minimally meaningful difference, sample size/power or feasibility precision, inclusion/exclusion criteria, repeated-measures model, missing-data rules and multiplicity treatment before recruitment. Report effect sizes and uncertainty, including null/adverse results. No sample size, percentage improvement or statistical significance is promised without evidence. Final success requires no unacceptable redistribution of load or safety tradeoff; numeric limits and stop rules require institutional approval.

Sex/gender and diversity: consider body dimensions, strength and fit rather than assuming one male body model. Plan recruitment feasibility and fit ranges with the research institution, and analyse relevant covariates where data support it. Identify exclusions transparently; do not claim universal fit from a small feasibility sample.

Open science: maintain a versioned protocol, metadata, analysis scripts and reproducible reporting. Plan open publications and share non-sensitive models/data where consent, IP and safety permit. Human records are pseudonymised with controlled access; raw identifiable records are not automatically open. Prepare a data-management plan and results/data access classifications with partners. Negative results are useful research outputs.

TRL: use a component-by-component baseline with dated evidence, owner and test environment. Do not assign an entry TRL from the concept alone. The proposed endpoint is TRL 4 laboratory validation of the new architecture. Higher-readiness background is explicitly separate. On-site operation, certification, market-ready production and clinical injury-prevention evidence are outside the promised endpoint.

## 2. Impact

### 2.1 Pathways towards impact

The immediate outputs are research knowledge: load-transfer models, experimentally characterised isolation/interface concepts, a laboratory demonstrator and a controlled comparison dataset. If favourable, these could enable follow-on industrial development of worker-assistance systems. Intended users of results include exoskeleton engineers, tool/interface designers, biomechanics researchers and industrial/construction employers assessing suitability. These are target groups, not confirmed purchasers.

The pathway is: research evidence → design choices and independent replication → later qualification and industrial validation → potential uptake. Reduced physical burden, improved task control and broader accessibility are hypotheses to test. Lower injury rates, reduced absence, monetary savings and productivity gains require later longitudinal/industrial evidence; no such figures are claimed here.

European relevance would come from transferable industrial design knowledge, research/engineering collaboration and future European manufacturing opportunities. UK construction access remains an existing concept direction; Italian and Polish roles are proposed capabilities. The September geographic priorities remain commercial hypotheses, not territorial exclusivity. Any future exploitation outside associated countries must be considered against EU-interest obligations.

Key barriers are unfavourable load redistribution, poor fit/mobility, vibration amplification, lack of IP access, insufficient novelty and unproven industrial demand. Mitigation is early comparative research, stop/redesign gates, background access agreements and structured stakeholder feedback. Benefits are conditional on research results.

### 2.2 Dissemination, exploitation and communication

Proposed dissemination: peer-reviewed biomechanics/mechanical research; reproducible protocol and non-sensitive analysis resources; technical workshops with industrial users; and concise public summaries. Timing follows IP review and participant-data protection. Record outputs and actual engagement rather than inventing reach targets.

Proposed exploitation: maintain a key-results register recording creator(s), ownership basis, protection choice, required background and intended user. Evaluate licensing, supply partnerships or a joint venture only after partner agreement. Existing Morph/NOSMO background remains separately recorded. New results ownership follows actual contributions and applicable grant/consortium terms; no automatic 50/50 split or exclusive right is assumed. Account for the topic's result-transfer/exclusive-licensing restriction.

Proposed communication: explain the research question, laboratory scope and limitations with consented imagery and accessible language. Distinguish research demonstrators from certified products and avoid health-protection claims. No public release or partner-logo use is authorised by this draft.
